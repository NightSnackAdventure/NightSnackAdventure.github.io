Selected Publications

Thermal Conversion Efficiency in Sustained Pyromantic Manifestations: A Quantitative Analysis

Journal of Evocation Studies, Vol. 14, No. 3 (Year 18)

This study examines the relationship between Ether expenditure and sustained heat output in controlled fire evocations across varying environmental conditions. Through systematic measurement of flame intensity, duration, and ambient temperature effects, we establish baseline conversion rates for common pyromantic formulae. Results indicate that humidity and atmospheric pressure significantly impact efficiency, with optimal performance occurring in cold, dry conditions—counterintuitively, the environments where heat production is most critical. The data suggest that evocation wizards operating in northern climates may achieve superior thermal output per unit of Ether expended, challenging conventional assumptions about magical efficiency being environmentally neutral.

Resonance Patterns in Elemental Air Manipulation: Toward a Unified Theory of Atmospheric Control

Quarterly Review of Aeromantic Research, Vol. 6, No. 2 (Year 19)

Air evocation presents unique challenges due to the inherent instability of gaseous media and the difficulty of maintaining coherent spell structures in fluid environments. This paper proposes a framework for understanding wind and lightning magic through resonance theory, arguing that successful air manipulation depends not on force application but on harmonic alignment with existing atmospheric patterns. Field observations conducted during both calm and turbulent weather conditions reveal that skilled practitioners achieve superior results by "riding" natural air currents rather than opposing them. The implications for combat applications are discussed, with particular attention to positioning strategies that maximize environmental synergy.

Comparative Analysis of Summoning Dynamics in Controlled Combat Environments

Proceedings of the Adanesne Arcane Society, Vol. 2 (Year 19)

Summoning magic, while traditionally classified as conjuration rather than evocation, shares significant theoretical overlap in its manipulation of planar energies and temporary manifestation of Ether-based entities. This longitudinal study examines performance variables in ritualized summoning combat across 89 individual matches, tracking factors including environmental conditions, summoner experience level, entity type, and match outcome. Of particular interest is the learning curve demonstrated by both summoner and summoned entity over repeated interactions, suggesting a form of extra-planar memory or behavioral adaptation previously undocumented in conjuration literature. Fire elementals, specifically, show marked improvement in tactical coordination after approximately 15-20 conjurations by the same practitioner, challenging assumptions about the purely mechanical nature of summoned beings.
